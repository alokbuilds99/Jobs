2026: AI shows up in layoff data

US announced job cuts where employers cited AI (Challenger, Gray & Christmas).
AI was the most-cited reason for US job cuts five months running (March–July 2026), and technology companies announced about 139k cuts in H1 2026, up 83% year on year. US unemployment still held at 4.1–4.3% from March to September 2026: a “low-hire, low-fire” slowdown, not a recession.

Fast loss, slow gain
Lost jobs disappear within a few years. Replacement jobs usually take a decade or more, often arrive in different places, and need different skills.
Sources & notes. Unemployment: BLS Current Population Survey (1948–2026; 2025 = annual average, 2026 = Jan–Sep average); 1890–1947 Lebergott series (Historical Statistics of the United States); pre-1890 values are rough reconstructions shown for shape only. Recession dates: NBER Business Cycle Dating Committee (the 2025–26 slowdown is not an NBER recession). Sector shares and occupation counts: US Census occupational data (Historical Statistics of the US / IPUMS), USDA farm labor, AAR (rail), MSHA/EIA (coal), BLS OEWS. These are rounded approximations for visualization. PC impact: McKinsey Global Institute, Jobs Lost, Jobs Gained (2017). Outlook: World Economic Forum, Future of Jobs Report 2025. AI layoffs: Challenger, Gray & Christmas monthly reports (2026). New work: Autor, Chin, Salomons & Seegmiller, “New Frontiers: The Origins and Content of New Work, 1940–2018” (2024). COVID-19 global losses: ILO Monitor (2021). Historical figures vary by source; treat values as indicative.
